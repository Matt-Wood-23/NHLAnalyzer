What was actually wrong

The SOG model reported 63.6% over/under accuracy against a 0.500 baseline. The line was mean(y_true) — taken from the test set, and not a line any book posts — and at a 2.5 line roughly 72% of player-games go under, so the majority class is the bar. Measured properly the model was overstated about threefold, and in the first walk-forward fold a constant “under” beat it outright.

Separately, combined shots per game have fallen 12% since 2021-22, from 63.2 to 55.6. That is not a roster-size artifact — skaters per game is flat at ~30.7. An expanding window inherits the high-volume seasons and over-projects, and the error grows as the gap widens.

The drift correction stack

Three corrections, applied in order, now shared in models/count_model.py. Each one reaches further forward in time than the last — which is the whole reason there are three.

01

Recency weighting

Training rows decay by season age, half-life 0.5 seasons — a season-old row carries a quarter the weight of a current one.

reaches → the newest training season
02

Newest-season calibration

A multiplicative factor fit on the most recent training season, folded into the log-link intercept where it is exact: exp(b₀ + log f + bx) = f·exp(b₀ + bx). The saved pickle is already corrected, so serving needs no extra step.

reaches → the newest training season, precisely
03

In-season calibration

A serve-time factor fit on the season being played. The first two stop at the newest training season; the season being served is a further step down. Uses only completed games, so nothing leaks.

reaches → tonight

SOG bias fell +0.092 → +0.051 shots, and on the most recent fold +0.104 → +0.019. Saves needed all three: steps 1–2 left +0.68, which is almost exactly one season of drift; step 3 takes it to +0.36.

Three things that looked like wins and were not

Recorded so nobody re-derives them. Each survived a first look and failed a second.

Retroactive closing-line value

Backfilled closing prices on 91 games put the model 0.0053 Brier ahead of the market. The bootstrap CI is [−0.0195, +0.0088] — it spans zero.

Why it isn’t real: model mean P(home) was 0.500 against the market’s 0.538, in a sample where home teams won only 45.1%. A flat 50% forecast would have scored 0.2500 against the market’s 0.2533. The market’s correct home lean is what cost it.

Shrinking low-experience rolling averages

The obvious fix for noisy rookie features: shrink them toward the league mean. Bias got worse, +0.174 → +0.196.

Why it isn’t real: fringe players average 1.44 shots against a league 1.94. They sit below average, so shrinking toward the mean pulls them up — exactly backwards.

Adding log(games_played) as a feature

Cut low-experience bias 40%, from +0.174 to +0.104. Overall bias rose to +0.074 and Brier degraded at every line.

Why it isn’t real: trading a targeted bias for a broader one is not a fix. Documented as a known limitation instead — treat projections under ~20 career games as biased high.

Parlays: measured, and the answer is no

The case for same-game parlays is that legs correlate, so the true joint probability beats the product a book prices. For shots on goal it is simply false — even between linemates.

Books shade same-game parlays for correlation that is not there, so a SOG parlay is strictly worse than the legs singly. The one real correlation runs opposite to the folk wisdom: shots faced barely predicts the result (−0.02), so “over saves + team loses” has lift 0.91. The correlated pairing is over saves with his own team’s moneyline.
